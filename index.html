What You'll Discover

For decades, women were left out of the research that shapes medicine. The gap still shows.

Did You Know?

  • In 1977, the FDA recommended excluding women of childbearing age from early drug trials. It wasn't until 1993 that federal law required NIH-funded research to include women. NIH Office of Research on Women's Health
  • In a 2025 survey of U.S. medical students, only about 1 in 5 could identify a sex- and gender-based medicine curriculum at their school, down from nearly 1 in 3 in 2016. Research Square (2026)
  • Women are still underrepresented in research today. A study of over 1,400 U.S. drug and device trials found women averaged just 41% of participants, and in psychiatric trials, 42%, even though women make up 60% of people with psychiatric disorders. TIME (2024)

The Science Behind It

This isn't a wellness trend. It's traceable research.

Every optimal range in OptimizeHer is traceable to published, peer-reviewed research.

TSH

The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology

A 2023 analysis of over 134,000 adults identified the thyroid (TSH) range linked to the lowest risk of heart disease and death.

Fe

Canadian Medical Association Journal

2025 guidance on iron deficiency in women, including ferritin treatment targets based on symptom relief and health outcomes.

B12

Annals of Neurology

A 2025 study found lower vitamin B12 levels, even within the "normal" range, were linked to signs of nerve and brain changes in older adults.

HDL

European Heart Journal

Large population studies showing the HDL cholesterol range linked to the lowest heart risk for women, and that higher isn't always better.

Every range comes from peer-reviewed medical research, and every source is cited. If it isn't cited, it isn't in the app.
